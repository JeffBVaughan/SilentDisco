Puck mockups: three ways to wear a channel

Wired-headphone client for the four-colour house party server. Every concept carries the same guts: ESP32 with a LiPo charger, PCM5102A DAC with its own 3.5 mm jack, a 1000 mAh cell, one WS2812 LED, one channel button. The shell, the button and the clip are the 3D-printed parts, and that is what these sketches are about.

Printed buttons and how they feel

Floating cap on a tactile switch

The cap is a loose part with hooks; the switch does the spring and the click. Most reliable, zero tuning. Use a 12 mm switch with a 7 mm plunger so the cap has a stable centre.

Living-hinge button printed into the lid

One part, no loose cap. A 0.8 mm bridge in PETG flexes a few thousand cycles before it cracks. Works for the Badge's side buttons; too stiff for a 44 mm face.

Clear dome over the pixel

For the Cable clip. Print the dome in clear PETG with 0 % infill and a single top layer; sand with 400 then 1000 grit for an even glow. The pixel sits 3 mm below the dome.

Tolerances to print first

Before any full shell, print a 20 mm test coupon: a cap in a hole at 0.2, 0.3 and 0.4 mm clearance, and a snap lip at 0.4 and 0.6 mm. Pick what moves freely on your printer and bake it into the model.

Server housing

The Pi 3B lives in a printed box with a face that tells the room what is going on: one 16-pixel ring split into four quadrants, one per colour, each quadrant filling up with listeners, and the 0.96" OLED showing the join QR code so nobody has to type an address. The Pi drives both straight off its header, so there is nothing extra to flash. Ethernet and power come in the back.

Driving the face from the Pi

The WS2812 ring takes one GPIO (pin 18 with the rpi_ws281x library) and the OLED takes I2C. A small Python service polls Snapcast's JSON-RPC every second, counts clients per stream, and lights 0 to 4 pixels per quadrant. The D1 mini stays in the drawer unless you want the face to be a separate, detachable panel.

What the OLED shows

A QR code for the join URL fits the 128 × 64 panel at 2 pixels per module. It alternates every ten seconds with now-playing for the busiest channel, and shows the Pi's IP in small text if the network changes.

Heat and power

The 3B idles around 40 °C and streaming four FLAC channels adds little. Slots in the floor and back are enough. Feed the Pi from a proper 2.5 A supply; the Dock's chargers get their own feed so puck charging never browns out the server.

Start with the Dial

Print it this week, since it only needs the Pi and the parts on your bench. The Dock is the second version once the puck outline is frozen, because its slots are cut to that shape.

Decisions that change the model

  1. Halo, Badge or Cable clip for the first ten? Or Halo for the chest and a few Cable clips for the over-ear basket?
  2. Lanyard or clip? A lanyard loop costs nothing to add to all three.
  3. Volume button: yes, or hold-to-cycle on the one button and keep the shell cleaner?
  4. Number on the face: printed in, or a sticker so the parts stay identical?
  5. Jack orientation: cable exits top (towards the head) or bottom (tucks into a pocket)?
  6. Server box: Dial now and Dock later, or straight to the Dock?
  7. Should the Dock's slots also hold the spare wired earbuds, or is that a separate basket?
